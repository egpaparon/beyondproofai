Oklahoma Personal Injury

The other side started working on your case the day it happened.

Adjusters, investigators and defence counsel were assigned within hours. Most injured people wait weeks before they talk to anyone. That gap is where cases are lost.

The situation

You are recovering. They are building a file.

Call (918) 583-0121

Within hours of a serious collision, the at-fault party's insurer has opened a claim, assigned an adjuster, and in larger cases dispatched an investigator. Statements get taken while memories are fresh and injuries are not yet fully understood. Vehicles get repaired or scrapped. Skid marks wash away.

Meanwhile the injured person is in an emergency room, then at home, then worrying about work. Most people do not speak to a lawyer for weeks. The gap between those two timelines is where a great many cases are quietly decided.

What we handle

Flynn Law Firm has represented injured people in Oklahoma since 2000. Our injury work sits alongside a workers' compensation practice, which matters more often than you would think — a substantial share of serious injuries in this state happen to people who were on the clock.

FELA & railroad injuries

Railroad employees are outside the workers' compensation system entirely. FELA requires proving negligence — and, unlike workers' compensation, allows recovery for pain and suffering.

Describe it to us

Hurt while working? There may be two claims, not one.

If you were on the clock when someone else caused your injury — a delivery driver rear-ended, a technician hit on a job site — there can be a workers' compensation claim and a separate claim against the person at fault. The second one covers things the first never will. More on workers' compensation.

What a claim covers

Unlike workers' compensation, this includes what it did to your life

Medical costs

Emergency care, surgery, rehabilitation, and the treatment still ahead of you — future care is part of the claim, not an afterthought.

Lost earnings

Wages already lost, and reduced earning capacity where an injury changes what work you are able to do.

Pain & loss of enjoyment

The part workers' compensation excludes entirely. What the injury cost you beyond money.

Before you talk to an adjuster

Four things worth knowing

The first offer is an opening position

It usually arrives before anyone knows what your treatment will actually involve. Early offers are cheap for exactly that reason.

You do not have to give the other side a recorded statement

Your own insurer may require cooperation under your policy. The at-fault party's carrier is a different matter — and it is asking for a reason.

Do not sign a blanket medical authorisation

A broad release lets an insurer trawl your entire medical history looking for something to blame instead of the collision.

Oklahoma has a filing deadline

And separate, sometimes much shorter, rules apply where a government entity or a railroad is involved. Find out which clock you are on.
